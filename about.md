I am a Swiss National Science Foundation PostDoc mobility fellow in the Geomorph group at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. I am broadly interested in the co-evolution of landscape processes and biodiversity patterns. During my appointment at MIT, I use Hawaiian islands as a study system to understand how topographic changes within island shape plant diversity trajactories. Previously, I worked at the ETH Zurich for my PhD and PostDoc, I investigated how tectonic and geomorphic processes shape the plant biodiversity and turnover in the Hengduan Mountains, China. I did my master in the University of Copenhagen. In my master project, I investigated how human play roles in the taxonomic and functional diversity in Denmark.
